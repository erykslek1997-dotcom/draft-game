# Pantheon reel script

Pantheon Hardwood is an all-time fantasy basketball game with a deep, data-driven engine.
Its channels argue about who really belongs among the greats, using numbers, honestly.
Voice model: two references, blended.
- **Ben Taylor (Thinking Basketball)** for the argument: precise, every number compared, honest about limits.
- **JxmyHighroller** for the story: one big question, a named phenomenon, a villain take, a paradox, a bookend.
Taylor proves, Jxmy persuades. We do both - never a hot-take merchant, never a spreadsheet read aloud.

## 1. Pick the format

| Format | Length | Spine |
|---|---|---|
| **Myth check** (overrated / underrated) | 30-45 s | reputation -> one killer number -> honest "but" -> verdict |
| **Discovery** (forgotten / hidden player) | 40-60 s | "I was looking at..." -> escalating finds -> why nobody noticed -> verdict |
| **Puzzle** (when / why / how) | 40-60 s | a question that sounds simple -> options -> the answer with the number |
| **Before / after** (a game, a season, a move) | 30-45 s | number before -> the change -> number after |
| **Long profile** | 5-10 min | hook -> series frame -> strengths (picture, number, verdict) -> criticism, honestly -> context -> proof in another setting -> verdict |
| **Take check** ("The Oracle Rates Your Takes") | 30-60 s per take | read the take verbatim -> split it into its claims -> find the key word -> test each claim (with/without record, list of precedents, engine number) -> one comparison that ends the debate -> verdict label |
| **Big question** (essay) | 1-10 min | the question -> news peg -> villain take -> name the phenomenon -> paradox -> escalating questions -> concrete pair -> authority -> bookend answer |

One idea per reel. If you have two ideas, you have two reels.

## 2. The hook (first 1-3 seconds) - write 5, keep the best

Patterns that work (all from proven long-form, compressed):
- **Reputation vs. charge:** "Twelve All-Defensive teams. The data disagrees."
- **Count of nothing:** "16 seasons. 2 All-Defensive teams. Zero All-Star games."
- **Puzzle:** "The hardest thing about LeBron's peak is figuring out when it was."
- **Stakes + shrug:** "Down 27 at halftime in the Finals. Big whoop."
- **Contrarian answer:** "Everyone says it was the adjustment. There wasn't one."
- **The one question:** "How much is an NBA championship worth?" - asked in second zero, answered in the last line.
- **Villain take:** quote the hot take verbatim, then "Yes, you heard that correctly."
- **Paradox:** "Going against everything you learned in math class, six is greater than eleven."

Rules: a number or a contrast in the first sentence; no greeting, no "in this video";
the hook must be true on its own, without the rest of the reel.

## 3. Body rules

1. **Every number gets a comparison.** Never "+5 on/off" alone - "+5, top-10 defense with him,
   bottom-five without". Rank ("#10 of 2,439"), percentile, a famous name ("ahead of Dirk"),
   or a before/after.
2. **Name exactly what the number measures.** "His playmaking score is ahead of Magic's",
   never "he was better than Magic".
3. **One honest "but".** State the weakness or the data's blind spot in plain words
   ("good but not great scorer", "this metric misses help defense"). It buys the verdict.
4. **Quote the charge before you answer it** when the reel is a myth check.
5. **Escalate in discovery reels:** each find bigger than the last; first-person beats
   ("okay, that's impressive" -> "now I'm texting people").
6. **Narrate the picture.** Point at what is on screen: "watch the 2011 bar".
7. **At most one metaphor per reel** (Taylor's "basketball octopus" works because it's rare).
8. **Short sentences, plain words.** A reel line should be readable as a caption in 2 s.
9. **Context in one line:** the reason the reputation and the data split
   (crowded position, bad roster, era, injuries, small market).
10. **Name the phenomenon** in two or three words the viewer can repeat ("ring culture",
    "floor raiser", "empty All-Star"). Reuse it; it becomes the reel's handle.
11. **A concrete pair beats a table:** one player against one player, same claim, opposite
    reality (7 rings / 8 ppg in the playoffs vs. 0 rings / top-50 ever).
12. **Hang the reel on the news when you can:** a fresh result, award or trade opens a
    timeless question.
13. **Split a take into its claims** and judge each one; name the key word the take hangs on
    ("the key word here is *arguably*").
14. **Deadpan repeat, then the number:** "30 players." (beat) "Their record with him: 65-13.
    Without him: 8-6."
15. **Precedent list:** when a take says "never before", run the precedents one line each,
    oldest to newest, then say what the list proves.
16. **An everyday analogy for a mechanism** (roster = recipe) - one per reel, after the numbers.
17. **Authority sparingly:** one short quote from a player or coach that backs the thesis -
    quote it exactly and name the source.

## 4. The verdict

One sentence that the viewer can repeat. Then a question that invites a fight in the comments.
**Bookend:** if the reel opened with a question, the verdict answers that exact question,
ideally with the same structure as the opening paradox ("if 30 years made 6 equal 11,
the last 30 make 3 equal 6").
Take-check labels (Greek seasoning, pick one): **Blessed by the Gods** (great take) ·
**Worthy of Olympus** (mostly right) · **Mortal Opinion** (half true) · **Lost in the Labyrinth**
(wrong) · **Cast into Tartarus** (atrocious). Say plainly whether you agree, then invite replies.
Conditional verdicts are allowed ("if the jumper comes, all-time great; if not, an All-Star").
Ending pattern: verdict -> "Who's yours?" / "Agree?" -> Pantheon logo.

## 5. Facts - hard rules

- Every number comes from the Pantheon engine or its data files, or from a named public source.
  Write the source next to the number in the script's notes.
- No "best ever" / "only player" claim unless it was checked. Unchecked -> ask it as a question.
- Check names across sources before trusting an award count: diacritics (Jokić, Dončić),
  marks (Chris Paul†), name changes (Lew Alcindor -> Kareem, Akeem -> Hakeem,
  Ron Artest -> Metta World Peace), nicknames (Penny = Anfernee Hardaway).
- Compare like with like: a peak window against awards from the SAME seasons; a career against a career.
- Old eras (pre-1970) rate low in the engine; never call a 1950s star "overrated" without
  saying it is an era question.
- DARKO values are whole numbers in our data - say "about -1", never "-1.3".
- No NBA game footage, logos or photos in the visuals.
- **Before the game launches, never say "our engine", "the game" or "the model".** The peak
  score is **"the Pantheon rating"**; everything else is "I" ("I ranked every guard of his era").
  Public numbers (BPM, All-Defense, box score, All-Star selections) prove; the Pantheon rating
  ranks peaks where no public number exists. The description carries one line:
  "The Pantheon rating = a peak-impact score built from box score, plus-minus and defensive data,
  for every era since 1951. Coming soon: Pantheon Hardwood."
- Pool facts for trailers: 1,661 players, 10,963 three-season windows, seasons 1951-2026,
  20+ data sources. Never say "every player ever".

```
TITLE: <on-screen / upload title, <= 60 chars>
FORMAT: <myth check | discovery | puzzle | before-after | long profile>, <seconds> s
HOOK OPTIONS: 1) ... 2) ... 3) ... 4) ... 5) ...   (mark the chosen one)

BEATS:
[0.0-3.0] VO: "..."          SCREEN: <big text / number>      VISUAL: <chart, card, icon>
[3.0-9.0] VO: "..."          SCREEN: ...                      VISUAL: ...
...
DATA FOR CHARTS: <table of the exact values used>
SOURCES: <number -> where it comes from>
CHECKS LEFT: <claims still to verify before recording>
CAPTION + HASHTAGS: ...
```

Target ~2.5 words per second of voice-over (40 s ~ 100 words).

## 7. Motion (calm, Taylor-style - the numbers are the show)

- Allowed: fade-in, a short slide (max 40 px), a bar or line growing, a counter ticking up.
  Duration 8-15 frames (0.25-0.5 s at 30 fps), ease-out, no overshoot.
- Not allowed: bounces, springs with overshoot, screen shake, zoom punches, rotations,
  "slam" scale-ins, flashing, glitch effects, particles. One exception per reel at most,
  for the single biggest number (a short scale from 1.08 to 1.0 is enough).
- One thing moves at a time. A new element starts only after the previous one has settled.
- Hold: after text or a chart lands, keep it still for at least 1.5 s before anything else moves.
- Between beats: a hard cut or a 6-frame crossfade. No wipes, no swooshes.
- Charts: bars grow from the baseline in order, 3-4 frames apart, then hold; highlight the key
  bar with colour (gold), not motion.
- Captions change on phrase breaks, never word-by-word karaoke.

## 8. Layout (one line of sight)

- Everything a beat says lives in ONE group in the vertical centre band of the 1080x1920 frame
  (roughly y 520-1500): title, card or chart, numbers. No title glued to the top edge with empty
  space below it.
- Inside the group, related things touch: title 40-60 px above the card/chart, numbers right under it.
- The caption sits just below the group (not pinned to the bottom edge), leaving the bottom ~300 px
  and the right ~120 px free for the platform's buttons and description.
- Empty space goes around the group, never between its parts.

## 9. Brand

Pantheon Hardwood - pixel art x ancient Greek. Palette: night #1C1420, dusk #2A1E33,
marble #E8DCC4, gold #F2C14E, terracotta #C0603A, olive #7FA35A, aegean #5C8FC9, ink #F1E6D2.
Fonts: Press Start 2P (titles), VT323 (sentences), JetBrains Mono (numbers).
Players always appear as **face cards**: a head-and-shoulders bust on a 24x24 pixel grid with
real skin tone, hair and one signature trait (goggles, headband, dyed hair...), team-colour
jersey, position badge top-left, jersey number top-right, name plate below. Never full-body
silhouettes, never photos. A hidden player = the same card with a gold "?" over the face. Greek words are seasoning, not the meal:
one per reel at most ("enters the Pantheon", "the Oracle says").
